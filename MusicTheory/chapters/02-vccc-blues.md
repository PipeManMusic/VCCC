## Theoretical Analysis

### 1. Quick-to-IV and Chromatic Diminished Passing
Unlike standard folk blues, jazz blues moves to **Bb7** ($IV^7$) in bar 2, returns to **F7** ($I^7$), and uses **Bdim** ($\sharp\text{IV}^\circ$) in bar 6. **Bdim** shares the identical tritone ($D$ – $A\flat$) with **Bb7**, operating as an ascending root movement that resolves smoothly into **F7/C** or root position **F7** in bar 7.

### 2. The VI7 to ii-V Turnaround
In bar 8, **D7** ($VI^7$) replaces the static $I$ chord, acting as a secondary dominant to **Gm7** ($\text{ii}^7$). This converts the final four measures into a cycle-of-fourths engine ($\text{Gm7} \to \text{C7} \to \text{F7}$) that keeps the harmony in constant forward motion.

### 3. Bebop Phrasing & Enclosures
Bars 10–11 highlight chromatic linear enclosures ($D\flat$ – $D\natural$, $G\sharp$ – $A$) to target chord tones on downbeats. Closed shell voicings on strings 6, 4, and 3 allow the player to comp rhythmically while holding position within the 5th-to-8th fret corridor.
